# Summary

The paper investigates how to automatically transform **Natural Language requirements** into **correct and verifiable ACSL specifications**, addressing limited research on **NL → ACSL**, **NL ambiguity**, data scarcity, and LLM generation errors. It proposes the **NL2ACSL multi-agent framework**, which consists of **Analyzer, Retriever, Generator, and Evaluator** and combines **requirement decomposition, RAG, CoT, and reward-based evaluation** to generate and select **ACSL candidates**, evaluated on **ACSLBench (598 C programs)** using **Frama-C**. The results show **Verified@1 rates of 74.4% with GPT-4o and 69.5% with DeepSeek-V3**, with improvements in **specification quality** and **semantic consistency**, while limitations include **NL ambiguity, dependence on underlying LLM capabilities, insufficient support for complex predicates/lemmas, and computational overhead**.

| **14. Evaluation and Results**            | Metrics include **Verified@1, SCORE, Accept, BLEU, ROUGE-L, and KEY/Key-Match**. The complete NL2ACSL framework achieves **Accept rates of 75.7% for GPT-4o and 74.9% for DeepSeek-V3**.                                                                              |
| **15. Evaluation — Verification**         | **Yes.** The generated ACSL specifications are formally verified using **Frama-C 30.0 + WP**. Under the **NL2ACSL framework**, GPT-4o achieves **Verified@1 = 445/598 (74.4%)**, while DeepSeek-V3 achieves **416/598 (69.5%)**.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **16. Evaluation — Human Evaluation**     | **Yes, for evaluator training.** Domain experts provide **preferred / dispreferred** preference annotations for generated ACSL candidates to train the reward model; during actual generation, the **Evaluator** automatically evaluates and selects candidates.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **17. Evaluation — Ablation**             | **Yes.** Removing **RAG** reduces GPT-4o from **74.4% → 67.2%** and DeepSeek-V3 from **69.5% → 66.7%**. | **18. Evaluation — Efficiency**           | **Yes.** Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-fma: **9s → 96s**; Llama3.1-8B-Tulu-3-fma: **13s → 101s**. The paper reports additional **computational overhead** introduced by NL2ACSL and identifies efficiency optimization as future work.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **19. Baselines**                         | **Zero-shot, Few-shot, and CoT**; as well as fine-tuned **Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-fma** and **Llama3.1-8B-Tulu-3-fma**.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **20. Limitations**                       | Dependence on underlying **LLM capabilities**; **NL ambiguity** may cause error propagation; the model may generate hallucinated/invalid ACSL functions, such as `\gcd`; complete real-world specifications may require many **predicates, lemmas, and other logical constructs**. In addition, the framework introduces computational overhead.                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **21. Missing Capabilities**              | Explicit future work includes **automated synthesis of auxiliary predicates and lemmas**, expanding the ACSL knowledge base with **library functions, logical language patterns, and annotated examples**, and further improving efficiency.
